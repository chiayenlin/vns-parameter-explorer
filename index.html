1Which fibers does each pulse recruit?ModelLit

A (large myelinated) B (small myelinated) C (unmyelinated) Not recruited

Current and pulse width set the charge per pulse and therefore how many fibers reach threshold. Frequency and duty cycle do not change which fibers are recruited by a single pulse — they change how often and for how long. The dots and bars are illustrative only: they show the direction of change — more current or longer pulses recruit more fibers, and fibrosis raises the current needed — not how many fibers are recruited. They come from a generic strength–duration (Weiss) sketch whose parameters were chosen for this page, so no percentages are shown. C fibers stay largely unrecruited in the clinical range; they are not required for the antiseizure effect in rats (Krahl 2001).

2Strength–duration: current needed vs pulse widthModelManual targets

Illustrative curves (same sketch as above, not fitted to patient data) showing the current at which about half of each fiber group would be recruited. Lengthening pulse width lowers the threshold, with diminishing returns beyond about 250 µs; beyond 500 µs extra charge adds little recruitment but drains the battery (Helmers 2012). The manual pairs target currents with pulse width: 1.5 mA at 500 µs, 1.75 mA at 250 µs, 2.25 mA at 130 µs.

3What the nerve actually receivesManual

Top: one second of the ON period — pulse height is current, pulse width is drawn exaggerated, spacing is 1/frequency. Bottom: one full cycle. At ≥ 10 Hz each train has a 2 s ramp-up and 2 s ramp-down, and the manual counts them: duty cycle = (ON + 4 s) ÷ (ON + OFF). Below 10 Hz there is no ramp. The manual does not recommend ON time longer than OFF time (Table 32); Helmers 2012 additionally cautions against duty cycles above 50% combined with ≥ 50 Hz, which SenTiva cannot deliver (maximum programmable frequency 30 Hz).

Both raise the duty cycle with time instead of current. Evidence that either beats standard cycling is weak: the AAN 2013 guideline rated rapid vs standard cycling Level U; DeGiorgio 2005 found three duty-cycle paradigms equally effective; in NOR-current, duty cycle was not a significant predictor and response rates did not differ between cycling types. Cells marked R and S in the duty-cycle table set these combinations directly.

Note: the R cells and the settings above are the NOR-current steps only, not a definition. The AAN 2013 guideline defines rapid cycling as 7 s ON / 30 s OFF (7 s / 0.5 min, about 30% with ramps), which NOR-current did not list; other short-ON, short-OFF combinations (OFF ≤ 0.8 min) are also commonly called rapid cycling and share the same AutoStim restriction.

Not for clinical programming. This page illustrates published relationships for teaching. Fiber recruitment is a simplified model, not patient-specific; follow the manufacturer's manual and clinical judgment.
