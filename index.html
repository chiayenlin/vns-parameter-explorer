1. Which fibers does each pulse recruit?

A (large myelinated) B (small myelinated) C (unmyelinated) Not recruited

Current and pulse width set the charge per pulse and therefore how many fibers reach threshold. Frequency and duty cycle do not change which fibers are recruited by a single pulse — they change how often and for how long. Thresholds follow a strength–duration (Weiss) relation, calibrated only to two anchors from Helmers 2012: about 99% of A/B fibers at 1.5 mA / 500 µs acutely, about 53% with chronic fibrosis. C fibers stay largely unrecruited in the clinical range; they are not required for the antiseizure effect in rats (Krahl 2001).

2. Strength–duration: current needed vs pulse width

Curves show the current at which half of each fiber group is recruited. Lengthening pulse width lowers the threshold, with diminishing returns beyond about 250 µs; beyond 500 µs extra charge adds little recruitment but drains the battery (Helmers 2012). The manual pairs target currents with pulse width: 1.5 mA at 500 µs, 1.75 mA at 250 µs, 2.25 mA at 130 µs.

3. What the nerve actually receives

Top: one second of the ON period — pulse height is current, pulse width is drawn exaggerated, spacing is 1/frequency. Bottom: one full cycle. At ≥ 10 Hz each train has a 2 s ramp-up and 2 s ramp-down, and the manual counts them: duty cycle = (ON + 4 s) ÷ (ON + OFF). Below 10 Hz there is no ramp.
